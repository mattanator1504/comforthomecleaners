# Humour

Humour is what keeps people reading past the first screen, so it's part of the job, not decoration. The method is the same on every site; **This site** at the bottom sets the flavour.

## How much

- **Blog posts: full.** A light beat roughly every second line, all the way through. Mostly small beats (a word choice, a bracketed aside), with one or two bigger bits per section.
- **Home, landing and service pages: light.** A grin or two per page. The offer and the proof do the selling.
- **Always straight:** the quick answer, FAQ answers, tables, prices, numbers, the call-to-action sentence, legal pages and forms. These get quoted by Google and AI, and they carry the trust.

## What works

- **The writer is the butt of the joke, never the reader.** Self-deprecation is warm and safe.
- **Root for the reader,** specifically. Not "you've got this", but something true about their situation.
- **Get petty about industry habits.** The quote that takes three weeks. The contact form that wants your fax number. Behaviours only, never a named company or person.
- **Brackets for asides.** (Yes, really. I checked twice.)
- **A topic joke in every section**, built from the actual subject, not one that could sit in any post.
- **One running bit per post:** introduced early, back in the middle, landing in the sign-off.
- **Specific situations, universal references.** Everyday moments, well-known sitcoms or sport. No niche memes or current trends.
- **Set up, land, move on.** Don't explain the joke. The useful sentence after it is what makes it work.

## Guardrails

PG-13 at most. No politics, religion, tragedy, health conditions or appearance. Never joke about the customer's actual pain (their leak, their injury, their debt). No guru or bro talk ("crushing it", "10x"). Never the same joke twice on one site.

## Writing a blog post with it

1. Write it straight and correct first.
2. Do a separate humour pass from the **bottom up** (the end of a draft is always the driest part).
3. Check every section has at least one line a stranger would smile at, and there are no three plain sentences in a row outside the straight zones.
4. Check the straight zones are joke-free.

**Opening:** the first 50 words land a specific observation, a slightly-too-folksy metaphor or a self-aware aside, then get to the answer.
**Ending:** a line that makes them smile, then a plain, clear next step.
